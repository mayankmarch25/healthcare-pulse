India's health burden has pivoted away from infections and nutritional deficiencies toward chronic and mental health conditions, while respiratory threats remain stubbornly on top. This is both a warning and a massive opportunity for Indian enterprise.

1990 vs 2021: a tale of two health profiles

In 1990, the top killers were maternal and neonatal complications, enteric infections, and TB and respiratory infections. By 2021, it was respiratory infections and TB (COVID-amplified), cardiovascular diseases, and persistent maternal and neonatal conditions. Surging up the ranks: diabetes and chronic kidney disease, cancers, musculoskeletal disorders, and mental health. Declining: nutritional deficiencies, malaria, and traditional infectious diseases. India is experiencing the West's chronic disease transition in fast-forward mode.

Where Indian enterprise must move

1

Population-scale chronic care

Digital patient registries, nurse-led care coordination, IoT-enabled remote monitoring for diabetes and hypertension at scale.

2

Respiratory health ecosystems

Next-gen affordable diagnostics, smart inhaler networks, air quality monitoring, TB and chronic respiratory disease aren't disappearing.

3

Cancer & rehabilitation networks

Oncology day-care center chains, AI-powered diagnostic imaging, musculoskeletal rehab platforms.

4

Mental health integration

Stepped-care delivery models, scalable tele-psychiatry, corporate mental health partnerships, no longer optional.

5

Digital public infrastructure leverage

Build on ABDM: automated claims, consent-based data sharing, AI-driven patient triage.

Who should lead this charge?

IT and engineering services (the digital backbone connecting to ABDM rails); medtech (localized devices with service contracts); pharma (integrated disease management and adherence beyond pills); hospitals (hub-and-spoke chronic care networks); insurers (outcome-linked benefits paying for BP control and diabetes remission); and startups (anchored in employer, insurer or state partnerships where outcomes are measurable).

This is India's healthcare moonshot moment. The companies that marry India's public health expertise with scalable chronic care solutions will own the future, building for a billion citizens and exporting proven models globally.
